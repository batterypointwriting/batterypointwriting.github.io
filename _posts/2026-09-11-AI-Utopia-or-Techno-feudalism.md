Recently OPENAI claims to have solved a part of one of the famous millennium maths problems;
of course this comes with a bit of controversy. OPENAI is accused of scraping the work of two
researchers who were close to this breakthrough, by using drafts that the researchers gave to
one of openAI's chat bots (Codex). This won't be a surprise to the reader who is probably fed up
from hearing about AI and at the same time worried about being replaced by it.

Within the past few months, I've come across clips of Elon Musk and Jeff Bezos (and other tech bros)
talking optimistically about how AI will increase productivity so much that we will have deflation,
where things become cheap and abundant, even to the extent that money won't have value (according to
Mr Musk), instead energy will be the currency of the future. They claim AI will solve humanities
problem, that is their justification for throwing all their resources to data centres,
while completely ignoring problems like climate change, social inequality and wars/corruption. Do
they really believe that AI will help humanity? Or are they just marketing it?

Don't get me wrong, technology and AI has potential to help and progress society but the details
matter. Yes AI will increase production, it may find novel technologies medicines etc... but who gets
those benefits? Who owns the robots, the AI models, the land? Who decides what AI factories make...?
There's no guarantee that the benefits of AI will spread across everyone unless governments and
institutions take action. There are two scenarios:

Utopia: AI productivity explodes, government tax the enormous capital gains(among other regulations), 
people receive social dividends, working hours fall from 40 hours a week to 30 to 20 maybe less. 
Healthcare, tech, travel become super cheap. People can spend more time on relationships, education, 
travel, cultural pursuits etc.

Techno-feudalism: AI productivity explodes, a tiny number of corporations own the models, robots, 
factories etc. Millions of people become economically 'unnecessary' and are at the mercy of 
corporations. Society struggles with unemployment and political instability (could write a whole blog 
about the rise of fascism in recent years). AI systems could track people and shut down protestors 
ruthlessly for example. We can have extremely advanced AI and tech and still have a very unequal 
society.

Interestingly, similar predictions have been made in history. John Keynes, who is one of the most 
influential economists of the 20th century, wrote an essay in 1930 arguing that by 2030 the 
cumulative economic and technological growth will increase productivity so much that living 
standards increase and people won't have to work as much (he predicted 3 days a week being 
considered full time) [1]. In the present we can clearly see that wasn't the case, we have much more 
productivity than his day yet people will work and work and yet struggle with the cost living. 
Clearly all the benefits are funnelled at the top while the majority struggle and dream of home 
ownership in developed nations; not to mention the people being targeted by AI systems in other 
countries... This is Capitalism. We know that corporations are greedy---a vial of life saving insulin 
costs around $2-$6 to make yet it retails for around $250 in the US for example.

So Utopia or Techno-feudalism? Depends on whether humanity can establish governments that serve 
the people.

**References** <br>
[1] Keynes, J.M., 1930. Economic possibilities for our grandchildren. In Essays in persuasion
(pp. 321-332). London: Palgrave Macmillan UK.
