I was told an interesting fact recently by a flying instructor; that the engines used on airliners are so reliable now, that an airline pilot will likely encounter an engine failure once every 185 years. 

So, looking to start my career as an airline pilot next year, my odds are looking pretty good!
 
Although, I’m sure people more skeptical than me will tell me that the risk is larger, and I could become very unlucky.

Maybe for the benefit of nervous flyers, these planes can happily fly on one engine, so I’m told, I’m about to start that part of the training. However, I have just been thinking about how interesting it is, that I will train, and continue to train (every 6 months, ish) on how to deal with something that I will probably never experience for real.

I am quite happy with this, both to train it regularly and to hopefully never experience it, and it makes sense to me to train the worst case scenario. Presumably, there are other jobs like this. Train crews must prepare for incidents, that may never happen to them? 

I assume surgeons practice for years, or decades even for operations they never perform, or at least do so rarely.

Surgery and flying require different skillsets, but maybe this idea of training hard, preparing for the worst, is just why we (humans), have managed to make some really dangerous things like replacing someone's heart or slinging a pressurised tube through the air at 500mph, actually quite safe.
